## Design Context

### Users
Single user: the reef tank owner. Uses the dashboard in two distinct modes — **ambient monitoring** (glancing from across the room, TV on, everything looks fine) and **active investigation** (troubleshooting an alert, checking a trend, researching why a parameter drifted). The interface needs to work beautifully in both contexts without being reconfigured.

### Brand Personality
**Curious. Living. Warm.**

Not robotic. Not clinical. The user is a hobbyist who chose this hobby because reefing is about color, motion, and the meditative quality of watching a living ecosystem thrive. The interface should feel like *looking at the reef*, not like reading a server dashboard.

### Emotional Goals
- **Primary: Scientific curiosity** — the data should invite exploration, tell a story, make the user want to understand what's happening
- **Secondary: Calm confidence** — at a glance, everything should communicate "your tank is healthy" or "this needs attention" without requiring interpretation
- **Tertiary: Stewardship** — a quiet sense of being in charge of something alive and beautiful

### Aesthetic Direction
The established color palette (bioluminescent cyan, kelp green, coral pink) and dark theme are correct and must be preserved. The problem is the *feeling* — currently too cold and robotic.

**The shift**: lean toward *reef garden* rather than *deep sea laboratory*. The lab metaphor brought rigidity, hard edges, and a technical/clinical feel. A reef garden has the same dark water and glowing colors, but with flow, asymmetry, organic shapes, and warmth.

**Key adjustments from current implementation:**
- More motion — things should gently breathe and shift, not just blink
- More warmth — use coral pink (#ff8796) more liberally alongside cyan; less cold blue dominance
- More whimsy — data presentation can have personality; not every number needs to look like a meter reading
- Livestock page especially: photos as the star, the data in support
- Avoid Grafana-style grid uniformity; embrace layout variety across cards

**Anti-references**: Grafana dashboards, Home Assistant panels, Apex Fusion itself (technical, utilitarian, joyless), any UI that looks like it belongs in an industrial control room

**References to aim for**: Something closer to a high-end nature documentary interface, or an app a marine biologist would *want* to use because it made the work feel beautiful. Warmth + precision together.

### Priority Pages
1. **Dashboard** — the "home" view, seen most often; must work at a glance AND reward attention
2. **Livestock** — especially with photos; this is where the actual animals live in the UI; should feel like a tank journal, not a database
3. **History** — for data reporting and trend investigation; needs to support deep dives without feeling clinical

### Design Principles
1. **The reef is alive** — use subtle motion, organic shapes, and flow to suggest the living system being monitored, not just numbers being displayed
2. **Data as invitation** — readings should draw the eye and invite curiosity, not just report facts; let the user want to tap in and explore
3. **Warmth before precision** — when in doubt, lean warmer; the user cares about the health of a living thing, not a machine's uptime
4. **Ambient then active** — design for the glance first; depth is revealed through interaction, not presented all at once
5. **Whimsy is allowed** — a dashboard for a reef tank can have personality and delight; it's not enterprise software
